# MVP Requirements — LHL App

## Status

MVP scope is being defined. This document records requirements already supported by approved product decisions; it does not authorize unapproved features, vendors, or implementation choices.

## Primary User

An adult resident—whether a homeowner, tenant, or another household member—living in a community exposed to recurring natural, environmental, or human-caused hazards, including wildfires, severe storms, floods, chemical spills, and explosions.

They already receive information through official alerts, weather applications, news, social media, text messages, and phone calls. LHL may surface available hazard information associated with their saved home address, with its source and limitations made clear, but it does not interpret the news or tell users what they should conclude. Its primary role is to provide a dependable way for residents to coordinate with nearby people they trust before, during, and after disruptive events.

During a disruptive event, the user needs to:

- Access available hazard and status information associated with their saved home address without LHL presenting its own interpretation as official guidance.
- Distinguish information requiring immediate action from updates that can be reviewed later.
- Communicate essential household status to trusted community members.
- Determine whether nearby people know what is happening or need assistance.
- Request practical help for themselves, another household member, their property, or a pet—including when away from home.
- Offer and coordinate help without contributing to message overload.
- Review non-urgent community needs without every interaction being framed as an emergency.

The experience must remain understandable under stress and usable by residents with varying levels of technical comfort. It should reduce the number of calls, texts, repeated checks, and competing updates required to coordinate with the community.

**Core user perspective:**

> “I call this community home, and disruptive events are an unavoidable part of living here. I already have apps and alerts telling me that something is happening, but I still need a dependable way to coordinate with nearby people I trust. I start texting and calling to find out who is around, who knows, and who needs help. If I’m away—or a family member, pet, or property needs attention—the uncertainty becomes even more stressful. I need one calm, dependable place to coordinate before, during, and after the event, without every update demanding immediate attention.”

**Primary job to be done:**

> When a disruptive event may affect my household or community, help me access clearly sourced information associated with my saved home address, communicate essential household status, and coordinate practical assistance with trusted nearby people before, during, and after the event—without adding unnecessary noise or cognitive burden.

**Product role:** LHL complements official alerts and information sources by supplying the trusted-community coordination layer. It does not replace emergency authorities, interpret the news for users, determine what users should conclude, or turn every community update into an alert.

## First Test Community

**Boulder Heights** is the working name for the first MVE-1 test community. It represents a broad, intentionally loose geographic area rather than a formally bounded jurisdiction, HOA, fire district, evacuation zone, or official emergency-management unit.

The primary pilot is one closed group of adult residents personally invited by John through existing trusted relationships. Most participants are expected to be homeowners, but property ownership is not an eligibility requirement because MVE-1's coordination mechanics apply equally to homeowners, tenants, and other adult residents. Membership—not a precise geographic boundary—defines the pilot group.

The group will participate in one sustained, six-week live pilot of the complete MVE-1 coordination model. "Live pilot" means real participants use the product over time; it does not mean waiting for or experimenting within an actual emergency. The pilot must exercise ordinary Ready-phase use as well as Alert, Active Incident, and Recovery behavior without depending on a real emergency occurring.

During the same period, LHL may run smaller controlled exercises using clearly identified test profiles and selected real-world incident conditions. These exercises may force specific states or interactions that do not naturally occur in the primary pilot. Simulated profiles, statuses, requests, and events must be clearly distinguishable from real people and real needs and must not create activity that could be mistaken for reports from residents in an affected area.

### Scheduled simulation exercise

During the six-week pilot, LHL intends to conduct one planned, time-bounded live drill in which a fictional disruptive event is introduced to the Boulder Heights participant group. Participants will interact with the app as real users while the event, alerts, profiles, and resulting conditions remain clearly labeled as `TEST`, `DRILL`, or `SIMULATION`. The drill will exercise otherwise infrequent Alert, Active Incident, and Recovery behaviors without waiting for an actual emergency.

The drill's scenario, timing, participant instructions, safety controls, measurements, success thresholds, and stop conditions must be defined in the approved Gate 6 pilot test plan. It must not imitate official instructions in a way that could reasonably be mistaken for a real directive, ask participants to enter unsafe conditions or physically evacuate, or measure whether participants obey LHL as an emergency authority. Participants receive advance notice that a drill will occur; whether its precise timing is disclosed is left to the approved test plan.

### Real-event rule

If an actual emergency, official warning, or materially unsafe condition occurs during the pilot, participant safety and official instructions take absolute priority. Participants are never expected to open, use, test, or provide feedback on LHL during a real event, and any use remains voluntary. A real WFIGS/IRWIN-eligible incident does not automatically suspend the pilot: John, as LHL CEO and the Boulder Heights pilot owner/administrator, has sole authority to suspend or terminate pilot operations. If the pilot continues during a small live incident, participant activity is observation and testing rather than reliance on MVE-1 for emergency warning or response decisions. Before participant access begins, the approved Gate 6 pilot plan and participant terms must define and test an easy manual `PULL THE PLUG` sequence, backup authority if John is unavailable or affected, participant messaging, audit behavior, and restart conditions. LHL must not declare an area safe, direct emergency response, or imply that it is an emergency authority. This rule supersedes the earlier automatic-suspension rule.

## First End-to-End MVE-1 Journey

Before a disruptive event, an adult resident receives a community-administrator invitation that clearly links to the platform download/install flow and separately provides a `COMMUNITY JOIN CODE` to save for later entry. The resident installs the pilot app, creates one independent adult User Profile, provides an SMS-capable phone number, successfully completes a one-time SMS verification through Twilio Verify, accepts the required approved-member Call/Text disclosure, saves home and assistance information, and enters the code. The code may reveal only the community name for confirmation and submits a pending membership request; it grants no group access. John approves the Boulder Heights request through a minimal role-gated administrator surface, after which the resident joins the private trusted group and reaches first-arrival guidance and the Home shell. Before approval, the resident remains in membership-entry/status/recovery surfaces; MVE-1 has no community-less sourced-Events mode. Multiple adults may save the same address, but MVE-1 does not link, merge, or deduplicate their profiles into a household account. Sensitive assistance information remains private unless deliberately shared for a limited period. Lightweight, skippable first-use guidance explains LHL's role and limits, notification behavior, and the 911 boundary during onboarding.

When an approved external source publishes push-eligible incident information or in-app-only advisories, watches, or other non-push conditions that can be associated with the resident's saved-home location, LHL may present the information according to the Gate 2 Event Matrix, with its source, timestamp, limitations, freshness, and correction state. Opening the app takes the resident to their saved-home context. On first arrival, skippable guidance identifies navigation, current sourced conditions, community membership, requests, the user profile, and tutorial replay from Help. Broader incident information and trusted-community activity are presented separately; LHL does not place the entire community into one phase or tell the resident what they should conclude.

The resident is away from home and needs help with a household member, pet, or property. They create a practical assistance Request within their trusted group. Another member sees and claims the Request using the contextual meaning `I'll help`. Claiming changes the Request from Open to Claimed but does not mark it complete.

An Offer is also claimable in MVE-1. Its contextual claim meaning is `I'll take you up on that`. Requests and Offers share the Open, Claimed, and Closed coordination pattern. Only the Offer originator who created it may release or reopen a claimed Offer to Open or close it to Closed; the claimant may not perform those lifecycle transitions.

Claiming opens the coordination item's private thread, where either the original creator or claimant may send the first message. The thread distinguishes draft or unsent, offline, sending, sent, and failed states honestly; Gate 4 decides whether and how outbound messages queue. For a Request, if the match is unsuitable, the claimant may release the claim or the requester may reopen the Request, returning it to Open without adding a fourth persistent state and notifying the other participant after the transition succeeds. When necessary, the adult who owns private household information grants the other participant access only to the sensitive information needed for that specific claimed Request or Offer, for a defined period using a one-day default. Unrelated entry instructions and household details remain private. Only the two participants can view the grant, and access expires automatically.

The helper decides independently whether they can act safely and consistently with official instructions. LHL facilitates coordination but does not direct the helper to enter an affected area or undertake unsafe action. The helper reports the outcome through the request thread.

The original requester confirms that the need has been fulfilled and manually closes the request. MVE-1 tests one in-app reminder after a Request has remained Claimed for seven days, asking the original requester to close it or keep it open; LHL never closes automatically. Saved-home status remains self-reported by each adult profile rather than inferred from device movement or inherited from another profile at the same address. As sourced conditions and home needs change, each resident continues to see their own saved-home context and relevant community activity without a universal status being assigned to the group.

After immediate conditions have passed, residents may continue coordination through recovery-oriented requests and offers. `Ready`, `Alert`, `Active Incident`, and `Recovery` remain internal working language for organizing the product and test coverage. Whether those labels appear to consumers—and what language replaces them if they do not—is deferred to Gate 3 design and the approved trust-and-tone principles.

## MVE-1 User Profile and Saved-Home Scope

MVE-1 uses one independent User Profile per adult. When its member record is created, LHL assigns one system-generated immutable internal `member_id` that anchors product records and future CRM relationships. The verified phone number remains a changeable contact/authentication attribute; replacing it never changes the `member_id`. An SMS-capable phone number and successful one-time SMS verification through Twilio Verify during onboarding are required. Verification establishes control and SMS receipt for that attempt but does not guarantee future reachability or delivery. LHL does not periodically or routinely re-verify an unchanged verified number. If a resident replaces the stored number, the replacement must be verified once before it becomes active. `member_id` format, generation, storage, code expiry, resend and rate-limit behavior, recovery, Twilio configuration, and later CRM integration remain Gate 4 implementation decisions. MVE-1 enforces unique phone-number assignment: a number already assigned to an existing account cannot create or be assigned to a second account. Show a number-already-in-use failure state with Sign in/use-another-number paths; existing-owner sign-in remains allowed. Enforce the same conflict check for a replacement number. Gate 4 defines normalization, authoritative/concurrent enforcement, privacy of duplicate responses and safe ownership/recovery checks. Reassignment, recycled-number disputes and any future exceptions remain separate discovery; no shared-number exception is approved. Multiple adults may save the same address, but their profiles, home information, requests, notification eligibility, and self-reported signals remain independent. MVE-1 does not link, merge, or deduplicate profiles into a household account. The profile separates resident-provided source information from system-derived display values. Residents do not author custom display names, household labels, biographies, slogans, or other open-ended identity content.

### Account, home and community separation

Founder-approved direction, 2026-09-09: MVE-1 remains one independent adult account/profile, one saved home and one trusted community (Boulder Heights). Future multi-home support belongs under that same adult account, with separately approved community memberships, rather than requiring another account for each home. The immutable `member_id` is not an address or phone number.

Gate 3 data/permission work and Gate 4 architecture must preserve that separation and a future extension path without building multi-home UI or selecting a detailed schema now. Multiple-home ownership and shared phone numbers are different concerns; this direction selects no sign-in or recovery method. Detailed home/community associations, privacy/sharing, notification subscriptions, switching and migration remain future work in `docs/future-roadmap-backlog.md`.

### Resident-provided source information

- First name and last name are mandatory identity fields. An optional preferred first name may be supplied.
- An SMS-capable phone number, successful one-time SMS verification through Twilio Verify, and explicit consent to approved-group Call and Text actions are mandatory for every pilot participant. The consent must explain that approved members may initiate native calls or texts and that the native application may expose the number to the other member or their device. Do not add a second optional self-attestation checkbox about receiving texts, a paid MVE-1 line-type lookup requirement, or periodic re-verification of an unchanged number. Verify a replacement number once before activation.
- A complete saved home address is mandatory and remains private from ordinary approved-group profile visibility. Address validation and provider selection are Gate 4 implementation decisions.
- Entry instructions are mandatory private home information. Door or gate codes, when applicable, are part of entry instructions rather than a separate profile category.
- Pets or animals, driveway or physical-access notes, utility-shutoff information, and mobility or other practical assistance needs are optional private fields in MVE-1. A standing field for people in the household relevant to an assistance request is excluded.
- Optional assistance fields may use replaceable starter examples, such as `breaker box located ___; gas shutoff ___`, to explain appropriate content. Starter text is never saved as resident data unless the resident actively replaces or adopts it.
- Homeowner-versus-tenant status, a general biography, a preparedness score, a standing equipment inventory, and a general medical record are not household-profile fields in MVE-1.

### System-derived values

- The internal `member_id` is generated once when the member record is created, is not user-editable, and remains stable across phone-number, profile, and saved-address changes. It is the durable association key for internal product records and future CRM relationships; it is not the member's public display name.
- The community-visible display name is generated as preferred first name, or first name when preferred name is blank, plus the last-name initial. The resident cannot enter a separate custom display name.
- A household label is generated from the saved address as street number plus street name, omitting the road/street type; for example, `25 Palamino`. The resident cannot enter a separate custom household label.
- Call and Text actions are generated from the required phone number, and the administrator badge is generated from the account's role.
- Exact roster layout, ordering, grouping, and placement of the derived household label remain Gate 3 presentation decisions. These decisions do not change the approved source fields or permit custom identity content.

### Deliberate helper sharing

Private household information remains masked until the resident deliberately shares it with the other participant in a claimed Request or Offer. The share confirmation begins with all applicable private fields selected; the resident may deselect fields that are unnecessary for the task, except the complete home address. The address is always included in a deliberate helper share.

The grant is limited to the private-data owner and the other participant in the claimed Request or Offer, uses a one-day default, expires automatically, and re-masks the shared information. The resident may revoke an active grant before its scheduled expiry; automatic expiry is the backstop rather than the resident's only privacy control. Every renewal requires a new deliberate confirmation and may never silently extend access. The product must briefly explain these protections at the point where residents provide and share private information. Expiry or early revocation removes the recipient's access; it does not delete the information stored in the resident's profile. Gate 3 defines the interaction and confirmation, and Gate 4 defines secure enforcement.

## Gate 1 MVE-1 Boundary Set

- Every approved adult Boulder Heights member may create and claim structured community Requests and Offers. A Request claim means `I'll help`; an Offer claim means `I'll take you up on that`. Administrator status provides no publishing, alerting, or emergency-communication advantage.
- No private preparedness-task or personal checklist concept remains in MVE-1. Structured preparedness checklists and readiness counters are excluded from MVP; possible administrator-uploaded static community resources remain post-MVP.
- The community hazard/route map is not an MVE-1 requirement. LHL does not maintain evacuation routes, hazard perimeters, or community preparedness guidance. If Gate 2 confirms dependable external incident data, MVE-1 may surface it in a clearly sourced non-map presentation; otherwise that capability is omitted rather than replaced with manually maintained current-safety information.
- Home/room floor-plan mapping remains excluded from MVP.

## MVE-1 Opening Sequence and Signup Safety

Founder-approved 2026-09-09 and refined 2026-09-10: welcome/role/limits (S01) → account/contact (S03) → home information (S04) → saved-home location review (S05) → notification explanation and OS permission (S02) → community joining (S07) → membership status (S08) → post-approval guidance/Home (S11/S12). The sequence does not end at notification permission. S02/S02b has one LHL action that opens the native OS permission decision, with no separate app-level `Not now` or defer action. Native Allow and Deny both continue setup; on Android, dismissing the native prompt without choosing leaves permission not determined and device pushes unavailable, but setup still continues. Later recovery uses the OS notification-settings path. Before continuation without a usable home location, clearly explain that both Event/alert notifications and the personalized Events feed are unavailable; community coordination becomes available only after membership approval under its existing access rules.

The existing signup safety explanation is fixed, not rotating. Rotating app-load messages are paused for MVE-1 and recorded in `docs/future-roadmap-backlog.md`; no message carousel or loading-screen placement is required. Existing safety instructions on incident details, request creation and Help/Safety remain required. Entry has two visible surfaces: not signed in → Create account / Sign in; signed in → saved starting screen within existing membership and suspended/revoked-access constraints. First-time users create an account; returning signed-out users sign in to their existing account, not a duplicate profile. No repeated signup for signed-in residents. MVE-1 uses SMS text-code sign-in; username/email-password setup is paused. Ordinary app opening does not itself require a new text challenge. Contact verification and sign-in are distinct. Gate 4 defines secure account binding, session duration/refresh, recovery and abuse controls; SMS ownership alone must not silently transfer an existing account.

X01 is a community-administrator email, designed to be readable on a phone, containing installation instructions and a separate `COMMUNITY JOIN CODE`. It is not membership approval and selects no email provider or automation.

## Saved-Home Location Recovery Coverage

Founder-approved 2026-09-09: present a usable interpreted address and map with `Use this location` / `Edit address`, including approximate-but-close acceptance and resident rejection of a wrong result. Draw distinct recovery variants for no usable home location; ambiguous/overly broad results needing clarification; temporary lookup failure with preserved input and retry; map-preview failure despite a returned coordinate; and failure to save the accepted location. A city/ZIP/region-only point is not by itself an established home location. Do not label a network or service failure as an invalid address, accept a rejected result, or imply a failed save succeeded.

Preserve input during recovery. Acceptance criteria must ensure an earlier lookup response cannot overwrite a newer edited-address result. Main paths belong to the next authorized A1 correction; detailed variants belong to Pass 2/A, reusing S05. The later same-day decision broadens the boundary: no usable home location means no Event/alert notifications and no personalized sourced Events in the feed. Explain both consequences before continuation; provide a location-needed state and address correction at saved-home/Events surfaces. S02 can still request permission for community activity without implying Event coverage. The complete address remains a mandatory resident-provided field, while establishing a usable matched or accepted home location is critical—but not a literal prerequisite—to the full approved incident-matrix Event/alert experience. Failure to establish that location does not block community Requests, Offers, claimed-item messages, or other approved community functions under their own access rules. Material rates of address matching, finding, or validation failure are a major product-functionality and adoption risk to measure during implementation and pilot planning. No provider, quality threshold, or success metric is selected here. Provider-specific quality criteria remain Gate 4; historical data retention is not changed.

## MVE-1 Device-Notification Boundary

Current policy: founder-approved 2026-09-09 O1/D3 supersession. The [notification eligibility workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1YjSBUpjJr9_YEPLnWssiF8ZB7Qw6pOIyL8SWgCK-gcA/edit#gid=20260909) is a working mirror, not a second canonical record: `Event Matrix` covers incidents, `Source Mapping` covers data sources, and `OTHER APP NOTIFICATIONS` covers the four approved community/direct-participant activities below. Sync affected mirror rules in the same pass as canonical changes; no automatic synchronization is implied.

- `Event` is the product-level and user-facing umbrella for a source-defined occurrence or official notice series. `Incident` remains internal/reference language when it accurately describes an actual occurrence; it is not a separate product hierarchy level. Each Event has exactly one Event Type, one stable source-defined identity, a current/latest state, and a chronological sequence of updates.
- Current approved notification placeholder copy is: `LHL complements official alerts by presenting qualifying information from approved sources; it never replaces official alerts.` It is a Gate 3 placeholder, not final copy.
- Different Event Types remain separate Events. For example, a Winter Storm Warning, High Wind Warning, and Special Weather Statement are not merged into an inferred master winter-storm Event even when they overlap in time and geography. Time, geography, or similar wording alone are insufficient identity evidence.
- Multiple attributable sources may contribute updates to one Event only when a shared stable identifier or a separately approved deterministic relationship establishes common identity. LHL preserves source and field/update provenance and does not average or independently adjudicate conflicting facts. Related Events may be linked without being merged. No administrator manually groups Events during MVE-1.
- Evacuation warnings and orders are prominent standalone Events with their own Event Type, authority, geography, stable identity, and lifecycle. They may link to a related wildfire or other Event only when authoritative identity evidence or an approved deterministic rule establishes the relationship. Boulder ODM remains a prospective source path rather than an assumed or approved source.
- Newly created community Requests and Offers remain visible inside the app and are permitted to generate device notifications for approved members of the relevant trusted community, regardless of lifecycle phase. This supersedes the former in-app-only rule. MVE-1 still has no author-selected urgency flag; that distinct future concept remains in `docs/future-roadmap-backlog.md`.
- Community-membership workflow changes are permitted notification events, subject to actual OS permission: a new pending request may notify the authorized community administrator, and approval or denial may notify the requester. These notices do not expose community content before approval and do not create granular in-app notification preferences.
- Device notifications are permitted for direct activity involving the participant: another member claims the participant's Request or Offer; a new message arrives in a private claimed-item thread involving the participant; or a material coordination/access change involving the participant occurs, including closure or private-information access ending.
- Incident notifications match the participant's saved property address only, with no device-location fallback. MVE-1 converts that address into one derived coordinate point; it does not use a parcel or property-boundary polygon and requires no operator or community-administrator verification. After address entry, the resident sees the interpreted address and a static neighborhood pin and may choose `Use this location` or `Edit address`. An approximate-but-close result is acceptable for the inclusive 10-mile MVE-1 matching rule.
- If an address is ambiguous, invalid, or likely mistyped, LHL presents a suggested correction or asks the resident to edit it. A legitimate address that cannot be validated may still be saved. When the selected mapping service returns a reasonable coordinate, the resident may use it; when no usable home location is established, profile setup and community coordination remain available but neither Event/alert device notifications nor personalized sourced Events in the in-app feed are available. Use an explicit location-needed state and correction path, not a no-events result. Explain both losses before the resident continues.
- MVE-1 does not include a resident-adjustable map or satellite pin. A resident-confirmed movable home-point flow is deferred beyond MVE-1 and will make later correction more precise. Gate 4 must select the exact mapping/geocoding service and approve API configuration, billing, permitted storage and refresh behavior, attribution, privacy handling, and retained geocoding metadata.
- When a notification-eligible source supplies authoritative affected geography, including a polygon, zone, perimeter, or address-level designation, use that source-defined geography. The approved point-only fallback is an inclusive 10-mile straight-line radius from the source-published incident point to the resident-accepted saved-home point. Calculate with full stored precision, display the distance to the nearest whole mile with no decimals, and add no hidden buffer; the published 10-mile rule includes the pilot's chosen tolerance.
- For Gate 2 evaluation, a `point-only fire report` is a source-published fire incident record whose only usable event-linked geography is a single coordinate or representative map point, with no usable incident-linked polygon, zone, perimeter, or address designation. The point does not establish a fire boundary, affected area, spread direction, evacuation area, or threat to nearby properties. This geometry classification is separate from notification eligibility: source, freshness, a stable source or upstream incident identifier, and the approved geographic matching rule must each qualify independently; otherwise the record fails closed to no push. MVE-1 does not invent a fuzzy composite identity for point-based notification.
- Geographic relevance and source/record eligibility are independent notification gates. An address match cannot overcome inadequate source access or permission, reliability, attribution, freshness, identity or deduplication, correction, or lifecycle behavior; source eligibility cannot overcome failure to match the saved address under an approved geographic rule. Both gates must pass before a device notification is eligible.
- MVE-1 uses a tiered fire-geography hierarchy: use qualifying source-authoritative, event-linked geography whenever it is available; when an otherwise eligible fire record is point-only, use the approved inclusive 10-mile universal radius; and send no device notification when either the source/record gate or geographic gate fails. Better qualifying source geometry replaces the temporary radius for subsequent address evaluation. Newly included households receive their first incident notification; previously notified households that become excluded receive a qualified update that is explicitly not an all-clear. Source/class-specific point radii are deferred beyond MVE-1.
- Conflicting point records are resolved programmatically only when they can be confidently linked to the same incident and the pre-approved source hierarchy and record-class rules clearly govern the comparison. Ambiguous identity, correction state, timestamp order, spatial meaning, or hierarchy applicability fails closed for a new point-based push; LHL never averages coordinates.
- Advisories are not evacuation warnings or mandatory evacuation orders. Under the current working rule they remain silent in-app unless an event/source matrix row is separately approved for push.
- Gate 3 starts from programmatic incident-type wording, source attribution, whole-mile displayed distance, the published radius rule, an explicit point-proximity limitation, and an in-app source link. Final copy and UX/UI layout remain Gate 3 work. For the approved WFIGS/IRWIN MVE-1 path, a previously unseen wildfire record is initially notification-eligible only when LHL first detects its stable `IRWINID` through a successful WFIGS query no later than 30 minutes after IRWIN `CreatedOnDateTime`, and every other approved gate passes. LHL stores this first successful observation as `first_seen_at`. `FireDiscoveryDateTime` remains the reported discovery/confirmation time for participant context, audit, and latency analysis; it does not start or disqualify the 30-minute notification clock.
- WFIGS is the MVE-1 public polling surface and IRWIN is its upstream incident source. LHL polls WFIGS on a consistent five-minute interval without depending on the WFIGS refresh phase. WFIGS states that it refreshes from IRWIN every five minutes, so two unsynchronized five-minute cycles imply nominal first detection in less than about ten minutes plus processing after IRWIN record creation; this is an engineering expectation, not a guaranteed service level. More frequent WFIGS polling cannot remove latency that occurs before the IRWIN record is created.
- The WFIGS path fails closed after 15 minutes without a successful query. Mutable incident fields older than 60 minutes from the applicable source modification time retain an honest timestamp but are not presented as current state and do not escalate a notification.
- Sources are not equally eligible to generate notifications. Gate 2 must confirm that a notification-eligible source provides reliable, attributable, machine-readable records with sufficient geography, classification, timestamps, stable event/update identity, and correction/cancellation behavior. Primary authoritative sources are preferred. A secondary source or aggregator may qualify only if it preserves upstream attribution and independently meets approved reliability and access requirements; otherwise it may appear as additional in-app information only. LHL does not adjudicate conflicting agency claims or create its own interpretation.
- Gate 2 must produce a founder-approved, expandable incident-notification eligibility matrix covering every candidate event type exposed by sources evaluated for MVE-1 and the Boulder Heights pilot. For each event/source combination it records push eligibility, silent in-app eligibility, recognized standard and hierarchy, push threshold, geographic matching, update behavior, separate acknowledgement eligibility, source mapping, decision status, and evidence/limitations. Unknown or unmapped combinations default to `Push Eligible: No`; a source does not define LHL policy merely because it offers a feed.
- No app-defined quiet hours, bundling, or rate limits ship in MVE-1; device settings govern quiet behavior. The OS is the only participant notification on/off control: MVE-1 has no in-app master switch or per-category preferences. Recommend enabling notifications, but request OS permission rather than assuming it is granted. Explain allowed, denied, and later-disabled states and provide a path to OS settings; denied permission does not block use of the rest of LHL. Pilot review should examine notification volume and ask why participants disable notifications; disabling alone does not establish the reason. Granular in-app settings are deferred until after MVE-1.
- If relevance, source authority, freshness, classification, or matrix eligibility cannot be established, no incident notification is sent.
- Lock-screen notifications contain no personally identifiable information or sensitive request/household information. Request-related notices use generic language; exact copy belongs to Gate 3.
- Drill notifications must be unmistakable as tests. `THIS IS A TEST ONLY` and `For app research purposes only, this is not an active emergency` are the Gate 3/Gate 6 baseline, subject to final UX and pilot-plan approval.
- LHL is not designed to be the first incident-alert source. Incident notifications repeat clearly sourced information to open the collaboration context, must not imply that LHL originated or independently verified the alert, and must state that LHL does not replace official alerts.
- Corrections, cancellations, expirations, and authoritative boundary changes update the in-app event record. Gates 2 and 3 determine which also warrant a follow-up notification. LHL repeats the source's status and never independently declares an area safe.
- Incident acknowledgement belongs to one exact source-defined update, not to device receipt or passive viewing. For MVE-1, acknowledgement eligibility follows push eligibility: an initial notice or material update is eligible only when that exact update independently warrants an LHL push under the approved Event Matrix, whether or not a device actually receives it. A material correction or source update requires a new acknowledgement state and cannot silently inherit the earlier action; final wording and reset presentation remain Gate 3 design work.
- The sourced Events feed shows one current item per Event rather than every update as a separate feed item. Event detail shows the current/latest state followed by the attributed chronological update sequence. The interface does not label an Event or update as `push eligible` or `in-app only`; those remain internal Event Matrix and delivery rules.
- For MVE-1 coverage, Event detail offers the participant's acknowledgement action and a compact summary scoped to the currently viewed community. S15 provides an on-demand searchable/filterable way to distinguish `Acknowledged` from `No acknowledgement recorded` for the exact update without placing an unlimited member list inline or using a default `x of total members` compliance-style denominator. Event identity is independent of community membership, and the view must not expose membership or activity across communities.
- S15's location and separate-screen status are a provisional wireframe hypothesis rather than a locked navigation decision. Wireframes must test whether the acknowledgement view belongs on a separate screen, an expandable Event-detail section, a filtered member view, or another location; they must test findability, small-versus-200-member scale, selected-community context, and social-pressure risk. The underlying coverage requirement remains even if the visual pattern changes.
- Automated evacuation-warning/order coverage is a desired MVE-1 pilot capability, not a guaranteed prerequisite. The approved prospective path is to pursue county-authorized, technically supported access through Steve Silbermann to Boulder ODM's internal Everbridge-to-Incident-Map publishing path. Until access, permitted use, identity, geography, freshness, correction/cancellation/lift lifecycle, attribution, retention, and supported interface behavior are confirmed, Boulder ODM remains a candidate rather than an approved notification source. If the path is unavailable, MVE-1 proceeds with an explicit no-automated-evacuation-coverage limitation rather than using an unauthorized interface or implying coverage; unmistakably simulated protective-action data may be used only within the approved pilot test plan.
- Exact event classifications, source mappings, duplicate suppression, update identity, correction/retraction behavior, and follow-up-notification rules are completed through the Gate 2 matrix before Gate 3 notification design. Gate 4 selects the delivery architecture; LHL never guarantees delivery.
- MVE-1 includes no app-level `Call 911` or `Text 911` action. LHL does not automatically call, act as an intermediary, or transmit participant, location, request, or incident information to emergency services. The product instead uses contextual safety instructions during onboarding or before incident notifications are enabled, on relevant incident-detail surfaces, in request creation where LHL could be mistaken for emergency reporting, and in an accessible Help/Safety location. The instruction is not limited to an LHL-classified active incident and is not repeated as a blanket app-wide banner. The primary copy baseline is `Follow instructions from public authorities. If you believe you are in danger, move to safety and call 911 when able. LHL does not contact emergency services.` A shorter incident baseline is `Follow official instructions. If you are in danger, move to safety and call 911 when able.` Request creation states `Requests in LHL do not contact emergency services.` Gate 3 wireframes and testing finalize wording, hierarchy, accessibility, and placement without reopening the no-action boundary.
- The pilot's data intent is local-first and avoids a centralized participant-data warehouse, but this is not a zero-backend architecture decision. Gate 3 must preserve honest time-bounded-sharing and independent per-adult profile behavior; Gate 4 must reconcile it with the minimum server-side processing needed for scheduled source polling, APNs/FCM fan-out, enforceable expiry/revocation, operator suspension, and audit logging. Gate 4 must also approve platform secure storage, encryption at rest, authentication, and app-access protection for sensitive information retained on a device.

## Trust and Tone Principles

These principles govern product language, information hierarchy, and interaction design. They do not prescribe final consumer copy, which Gate 3 must design and test.

- **Coordinate; do not command.** LHL helps trusted people coordinate but never directs emergency behavior, tells a helper to enter an affected area, or implies authority over official instructions.
- **Source; do not interpret.** Show the source, timestamp, coverage, and limitations of incident information without telling residents what to conclude or presenting LHL's interpretation as official guidance.
- **Describe relevance carefully.** Associate sourced information with a saved home address without claiming certainty, verification, or geographic precision the source cannot support.
- **Reserve urgency.** New community Requests and Offers may notify during MVE-1 to test coordination value and notification volume; a push does not label an item urgent. Reserve emergency implications and urgent presentation for the approved sourced-incident treatment. Do not turn other unapproved activity into a push.
- **Use calm, plain language.** Prefer short factual statements, explicit timestamps, and clear actions over dramatic, speculative, emotionally loaded, or engagement-oriented language.
- **Distinguish every information state.** Official or clearly sourced information, resident-reported information, missing information, stale information, intentionally unshared information, and simulated content must never appear equivalent.
- **Make privacy deliberate and legible.** Explain why information is requested, the future user benefit it enables, what is shared, with whom, for how long, and when access ends. Do not imply that masked information was deleted when access merely expired or was revoked.
- **Put safety and official instructions first.** Never imply that app participation, helping a neighbor, completing a request, or supporting a pilot takes priority over personal safety or official direction.
- **Avoid social pressure and engagement mechanics.** Do not use streaks, popularity signals, reactions, public rankings, shame, or language that pressures residents to report, respond, help, or remain active in the app.

The founder-provided communication-saturation whitepaper is one perspective supporting discussion of reserved urgency, calm hierarchy, and reduced cognitive load. It is not proof of a universal or precisely measurable `Saturation Parameter`, and its claims must not be presented as settled scientific fact without stronger evidence. Its proposals for continuous coordinate tracking, mesh networking, automated cross-municipal synchronization, and sensor integration are not approved MVE-1 scope.

## Approved MVP Constraints

- The product must support the four resilience phases: Ready, Alert, Active Incident, and Recovery.
- Ready, Alert, Active Incident, and Recovery are internal working language for MVE-1 planning and test coverage; consumer-facing lifecycle labels and presentation remain TBD until Gate 3 design.
- MVE-1 is the first validation slice inside MVP scope, not a post-MVP expansion.
- MVE-1 must validate the approved lifecycle flows: phase change, community request, offer post, Active Incident regression, and Recovery request.
- Sensitive household information must use time-bounded access, expire automatically, and be re-masked after expiry.
- Each adult uses an independent User Profile. Sharing an address does not link, merge, or deduplicate profiles, and MVE-1 does not collect a standing field for other people in the household.
- Each member record has one immutable internal `member_id`. Product-record relationships use that identifier rather than the verified phone number, and changing a phone number does not create a new identity or rewrite prior record ownership.
- A user whose last successful server handshake is more than five minutes old must be represented as offline to their group.
- A community or Recovery Request or Offer uses exactly three persistent states — Open, Claimed, Closed — with claiming distinct from closing. A Request uses the claim meaning `I'll help`; an Offer uses `I'll take you up on that`. Claiming opens an inline thread visible only to the original creator and claimant, and either may send the first message. For Requests, a claimant may release or a requester may reopen an unsuitable match, returning the Request to Open without a fourth persistent state and notifying the other participant, and only the original requester closes. For Offers, only the originator who created the Offer may release or reopen it to Open or close it to Closed; the claimant may not perform those lifecycle transitions. MVE-1 tests one in-app reminder after a Request remains Claimed for seven days, asking the requester to close or keep it open, but never closes automatically. Claimed-item messaging distinguishes unsent, offline, sending, sent, and failed states honestly; Gate 4 decides queue mechanics.
- Gate 3 screen inventory includes lightweight, skippable progressive first-use guidance during onboarding and first household-home arrival, with replay from Help; final copy and layout remain design/testing work.
- LHL has no general group chat, open-ended direct messaging, neighborhood forum, social feed, or email-list replacement. In-app communication is attached to a concrete coordination object: a structured Request or Offer is visible to the trusted group, and private conversation is limited to the original creator and claimant within that claimed item's thread.
- Approved group-member profiles include Call and Text actions that launch the device's native phone or messaging application using the verified member-provided phone number. An SMS-capable number, successful one-time SMS verification through Twilio Verify, and explicit consent to these actions are mandatory for pilot participation. An unchanged number is not periodically re-verified; a replacement must be verified once before activation. The profile shows the actions rather than printing the underlying number, although the native application may expose it when an action is used. LHL does not auto-send, capture, synchronize, or guarantee delivery of native calls or texts and makes no claim that carrier or satellite delivery is available. Email actions are excluded from MVE-1; exact final disclosure copy is completed in low-fidelity design, while Gate 4 finalizes verification/authentication configuration and recovery mechanics.
- Offer Post supports an optional Item/Tool vs. Skill/Labor category tag, usable in any phase; no phase-gated flow split. An approved member may claim an Open Offer using `I'll take you up on that`, after which the two participants may coordinate in its private thread.
- New community requests and new offers follow the device-notification boundary above.
- Community hazard/route mapping is excluded from MVE-1, and home/room floor-plan mapping is excluded from MVP.
- Hazard/status location matching is home-address-only in MVE-1; no continuous background location tracking.
- MVE-1 supports three distinct, voluntary, time-stamped signals recorded independently by each adult profile and associated with that profile's saved home: (1) a minimal incident acknowledgement tied to one exact sourced update, using an optional deliberate action in the direction of `I saw this update` and expressing absence as `No acknowledgement recorded`—never `Aware`/`Unaware`; (2) self-reported home presence, distinguishing someone home, household away, not shared, and no recent update without geofencing; and (3) a self-reported incident check-in indicating that the reporting profile's household was not in, or has left, the area relevant to the incident, without presenting that statement as a verified declaration of safety. Profiles using the same address may report different states. Missing, stale, and intentionally unshared information must remain distinct.
- The minimal acknowledgement is the approved MVE-1 baseline for prototyping and testing. It requires deliberate user action and never proves awareness, comprehension, agreement, compliance, or safety. Acknowledgement eligibility exactly follows the approved Event Matrix's push eligibility for an initial notice or material update: push-eligible updates are acknowledgement-eligible, while in-app-only updates are not. Eligibility describes the exact sourced update and does not depend on actual device delivery. A material correction or source update must not silently inherit an earlier acknowledgement. Final consumer wording, prompting behavior, visibility hierarchy and audience, expiry/staleness treatment, and reset presentation remain Gate 3 decisions. A fuller visible acknowledgement is not approved as the MVE-1 baseline; John has identified it only as a possible later direction to compare against the minimal baseline.
- The product opens saved-home-first: available sourced incident information associated with the user's saved-home location is distinct from broader incident context, user-reported home signals, and trusted-community activity. MVE-1 does not assign one universal hazard status or lifecycle phase to an entire community.
- John is pre-provisioned as the initial Boulder Heights administrator. The MVE-1 administrator role is community-specific and limited to reviewing and approving or denying join requests. Administrator tools appear within the applicable Community. Administrators do not remove existing members in-app, moderate content or messages, set or confirm lifecycle phases, curate an incident narrative, add or validate hazard sources, interpret official information, maintain a transition log, or direct community behavior during an emergency. Administrator creation/selection, multiple administrators, role transfer, and unavailable-administrator recovery are deferred governance/continuity work. If pilot access must be revoked, the approved pilot operations plan must provide a manual operator-level safeguard outside the participant-facing MVE-1 feature set.
- Pilot membership onboarding uses three distinct gates: a community administrator sends a TestFlight or approved Android-testing invitation that clearly grants download/install access and separately supplies a `COMMUNITY JOIN CODE`; entering a valid preconfigured code may reveal only the community name and allows the resident to submit a pending membership request; and John's approval grants trusted-group access and the normal post-approval app shell. Neither installation nor code entry alone exposes member, request, activity, or other group content, and MVE-1 offers no community-less sourced-Events mode. MVE-1 includes participant code entry, pending/approved/denied states, and a minimal role-gated administrator request list with approve/deny actions. An invalid/bad code is distinct from a connection or service failure that prevents checking the code; the latter preserves input and offers retry. Denial names the community, offers resubmission through code entry, and provides a pre-addressed `Contact Community Administrator` email action without displaying the recipient address in the interface. The administrator queue scrolls vertically and orders pending requests oldest first. Each S09 queue preview shows the resident's full self-provided address and a `Verified phone` completion status, but never the phone number. The status means only that the required phone-verification step succeeded; it is not proof of identity, residency, administrator vetting, or human-versus-bot status. S10 likewise shows the address as self-provided and unverified while omitting the phone number. Address visibility is limited to the authorized administrator while the request awaits disposition; it is not ordinary-member-visible or an administrator-facing post-disposition history field. In-app code generation, rotation, expiration, invitation analytics, and general community-code management are deferred.

## MVE-1 Post-Approval Home and Global Navigation

Founder-approved starting direction, 2026-09-10: S12 is the post-approval Home screen and saved starting shell. A resident cannot enter this shell until community membership is approved. The header currently presents the LHL name/logo. On Home and Events, a shortened saved-home label such as `25 Example Lane` appears immediately below the header without city, state, or ZIP. The label is static in MVE-1 while preserving a future visual path for multi-home selection.

The Home body scrolls and contains three primary sections: an Event preview, a Community Summary, and Home Status. A fixed, equally spaced footer remains visible across post-approval in-app root destinations and uses labeled icons in this order: `Home`, `Community`, `Messages`, `Profile`, `Help`. Pre-approval/onboarding surfaces, native OS surfaces, and blocking confirmation dialogs are exceptions. `Home` returns to or reloads S12; it does not navigate directly to Home Status.

- **Event preview:** Show up to three current source-defined Events and a path to the complete Events feed. Each Event appears once with its latest timestamped state; Event detail contains the attributed update history. Different Event Types keep separate identities; any later decision about visually grouping related Events or how they compete for preview slots does not merge them. Exact ordering remains a separate product-hierarchy decision, except that evacuation protective-action Events must remain highest. The open internal `Event geographic match basis` workstream must distinguish source-authoritative affected-area claims from published point-proximity claims. Until that work is resolved, every S12 Event preview row reserves `[RELEVANCE — TBD]`; this is a design placeholder, not approved consumer copy or a selected visual treatment. This does not create a separate `Alert` entity or choose a JSON/implementation representation. If no usable home location exists, replace the Event preview with an explanation of both Event losses and an `Edit address` action; do not present it as no current Events. Community Summary, Home Status, and global navigation remain available.
- **Community Summary:** Show the community name, approved-member count, Open Request count, Open Offer count, and per-resident new/unviewed indicators. For the starting definition, `new` means created since that resident last viewed Community and clears as items are viewed. The summary opens Community and is not a social feed.
- **Home Status:** Keep Home Status on Home even without a usable mapped coordinate. Show the reporting resident's latest voluntary status and timestamp, with inline `Home`/`Away` controls and no freeform status field. Provisionally, `Home` means someone in that resident's household is home; `Away` means the resident reports the household away. Adults at the same address report independently. The two-state taxonomy is provisional and requires later review.
- **Messages:** Provide an always-present inbox limited to claimed Request/Offer threads, including empty and unread states. It does not enable general direct messages, a member-directory conversation feed, or group chat.
- **Profile and Help:** Profile contains the onboarding identity/contact, address/access, optional home information, and community membership. Leaving a community remains future scope. Notification Settings reports actual OS status and opens native OS settings; it is not an in-app switch. Help retains safety/911, privacy, tutorial replay, manual concern reporting, and a notification explanation linking to the same settings route.
- **Navigation boundary:** S11 is the final footer-free onboarding screen. The global footer first appears on S12, then remains visible on post-approval root destinations subject to the already-approved native-OS and blocking-dialog exceptions.
- MVE-1 supports exactly one trusted community group: Boulder Heights. A pilot participant cannot create, join, switch among, or receive activity from multiple groups.
- The selected authentication/backend approach must provide a protected operator-level way to revoke previously approved access before pilot credentials are issued, even though MVE-1 has no participant-facing member-removal feature.
- Sourced incident context must avoid implying official precision unless the source, coverage, and limitations are clear.
- The app must not require a backend, authentication, notification, or external-data provider until that choice is separately approved and recorded.
